Cross-Site Synthesis

Turning an archive into a network, instead of just a longer list
What this is: An ordinary topic site answers "what is X." A synthesis essay answers "what X actually is in each of my sites, and why those versions don't line up." Every source is a page already published on this hub, and every section links straight back to it — so readers can drill down, and the archive becomes a network.

Selection Criteria

A concept appearing across sites is not by itself a reason to write about it. All four must hold:

  • The sweet spot is 4–8 sites. A concept spanning 15+ sites (risk, decision-making) inevitably turns to mush — a sentence per site, depth nowhere. Fewer than 4 and there is no network to draw.
  • The "machines" really do differ — the same word sits on genuinely different mechanisms, not one idea reworded.
  • There is a sharp hook: either a break (where the analogy fails) or a lens gap (several disciplines each seeing a different layer of one object).
  • The source material is real: at least 3 sites with full pages on it, not passing mentions.

Three Genres

  • A · One word, different machines — the word travelled to another field and the machinery didn't follow. The point is to say exactly where it breaks.
  • B · One object, multiple lenses — several disciplines each see one layer of the same thing. The point is where the lenses complement and where they collide.
  • C · One method, reused across domains — a single reasoning tool, and the different standards of evidence each field demands of it.
